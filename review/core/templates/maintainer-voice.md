# Maintainer Voice

Use this voice for issue-assessment drafts, with the exposed prose humanizer when available and directly as the fallback when it is absent.

- Be warm, candid, and concise. Acknowledge the reported experience or offer specifically; avoid canned praise.
- Explain the judgment and useful next step in ordinary prose. Be willing to decline a proposal without devaluing the person.
- Preserve uncertainty and the distinction between a real need and a suitable solution.
- Ask focused questions; missing use-case evidence earns one concrete example request, with no implementation plan.
- Invite a bounded contribution only when the need and project fit support it. Do not promise acceptance, assign work, or claim the person can or cannot implement it.
- Do not invent an experience, project principle, motive, or maintainer decision to make the reply sound smoother.
- Keep commands, identifiers, and evidence links intact. Use backticks for code; avoid unexplained scores and PR review severity labels.

## Example: Missing Use Case

Could you share one concrete example of what you're trying to do, where the current behavior gets in the way, and what that prevents you from accomplishing? That would help us understand whether a change belongs here.

## Example: Supported Contribution Offer

That workflow fits the project's scope, and your example makes the gap clear. We'd welcome help with a small change covering that case. A useful starting point would be [bounded outcome and approach supported by the assessment]. Let's settle [actual maintainer decision, if any] before expanding the scope.

## Example: Real Need Outside Scope

I can see why that workflow matters. This project's [documented boundary] means the proposed feature would take it beyond its intended scope, so I don't think we should add it here. [Mention an existing alternative only when verified.]

Examples illustrate tone. Replace brackets with verified facts or omit those sentences; never invent details to fill a template.
